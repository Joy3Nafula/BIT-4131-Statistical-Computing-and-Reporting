# BIT 4131: Statistical Computing and Reporting

A reproducible collection of statistical theory, mathematical formulations, synthetic dataset analyses, and Python implementations covering 23 core topics in statistical computing and reporting.

## Overview

Each topic in this repository follows a structured analytical workflow:
1. **Theoretical Explanation** – Core statistical concepts, assumptions, and use cases.
2. **Mathematical Formulation** – Underlying equations, estimators, and test statistics.
3. **Dataset Analysis & Implementation** – Reproducible Python scripts and Jupyter Notebooks (`.ipynb`) built in Google Colab.
4. **Statistical Output & Visualization** – Summary tables, model diagnostics, and statistical plots.
5. **Interpretation & Conclusion** – Practical translation of numerical results and hypothesis decisions.

## Tech Stack

* **Environment:** Google Colab / Jupyter Notebook (`.ipynb`)
* **Core Libraries:** `numpy`, `scipy`, `pandas`, `statsmodels`, `scikit-learn`, `matplotlib`, `seaborn`

## Topics Covered

1. **Measures of Central Tendency and Dispersion** – Mean, median, mode, variance, standard deviation, and IQR.
2. **Probability Theory and Conditional Probability** – Axioms of probability, joint/marginal distributions, and Bayes' theorem.
3. **Probability Distributions** – Normal, Binomial, Poisson, and Exponential distributions (PDF, PMF, CDF).
4. **Sampling Methods and Central Limit Theorem (CLT)** – Simple random/stratified sampling and sampling distribution convergence.
5. **Estimation and Confidence Intervals** – Point estimates, standard error, and 95% confidence interval construction.
6. **Hypothesis Testing** – One-sample and two-sample t-tests, p-values, Type I/II errors, and statistical significance.
7. **Correlation and Covariance** – Pearson and Spearman correlation coefficients, covariance matrices, and scatter analysis.
8. **Simple and Multiple Linear Regression** – Ordinary Least Squares (OLS), $R^2$, residual diagnostics, and parameter inference.
9. **Matrix Algebra for Statistical Computing** – Matrix multiplication, transpose, inverse, determinants, and solving linear systems.
10. **Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, and PCA** – Spectral decomposition, explained variance ratio, and dimensionality reduction.
11. **Numerical Optimization** – Objective function minimization and gradient-based convergence (BFGS).
12. **Random Numbers and Monte Carlo Methods** – Stochastic simulation, $\pi$ estimation, and Law of Large Numbers convergence.
13. **Time-Series Analysis** – Additive decomposition (trend, seasonality, residuals), ACF plots, and Holt-Winters forecasting.
14. **Statistical Data Visualization** – Histograms, boxplots, scatter plots, and multi-panel line graphs.
15. **Data Transformation and Normalization** – Log transformations, Z-score standardization, and Min-Max scaling.
16. **ANOVA and Experimental Design** – One-way ANOVA, F-statistic, and Tukey HSD post-hoc comparisons.
17. **Chi-Square and Categorical Data Analysis** – Contingency tables, expected frequencies, $\chi^2$ test of independence, and mosaic plots.
18. **Logistic Regression** – Sigmoid probabilities, log-odds, odds ratios, confusion matrices, and classification metrics.
19. **Non-Parametric Statistical Methods** – Rank-based Mann-Whitney U test vs. parametric t-test on skewed data.
20. **Bootstrap and Resampling Methods** – Sampling with replacement, empirical standard errors, and percentile confidence intervals.
21. **Multivariate Statistical Analysis** – Multivariate mean vectors, covariance structures, PCA scores, and K-Means clustering.
22. **Missing-Data Analysis and Imputation** – Missingness heatmaps, `SimpleImputer` mean imputation, and pre/post distribution checks.
23. **Statistical Reporting and Reproducible Analysis** – End-to-end automated reporting pipeline combining code, tables, plots, and conclusions.

## Viewing & Running the Notebooks

* **On GitHub:** Click any `.ipynb` file in the repository to view rendered Markdown explanations, formulas, code, and embedded output plots directly in the browser.
* **In Google Colab:** Open any notebook in Google Colab and select **Runtime > Run all** to reproduce all synthetic datasets and outputs.
